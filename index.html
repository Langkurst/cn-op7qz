SwimSorted

Website and demo live. No customers yet.

My own product for pool technicians and small pool service teams, started in October 2026. It is built on my work servicing pools.

A technician enters a water test and the app works out each dose from the products they carry. Every dose has a safety limit, and acid and chlorine never go in together. It also covers the day's pools, hours, visit records and van stock, and it works with no phone signal.

The product has no AI in it. It was built with AI coding tools. Company sign-ups open in December 2026.

KeaClip

Windows app, 12 public releases

A free Windows app for saving, trimming and sharing video and audio, with no ads, account or tracking. Released in October 2026.

It installs without admin rights and updates only when the user agrees. Each release publishes checksums, and a public list sets out 30 known limits. The last two releases could not publish unless every test and a 41-check installer self-test passed.

How I work with AI

The AI drafts and recommends. It does not buy, publish, send or permanently delete without my say. Passwords and keys stay out of logs and saved files.

Three examples from my project records. The dates are New Zealand dates.

  1. 14 October 2026

    A cloud service that could have charged me

    On KeaClip, the AI advised adding cloud storage and said a charge "isn't impossible in theory". I told it I must never be charged. It added hard limits, with tests, before the first upload.

  2. 15 October 2026

    A domain purchase I declined twice

    On SwimSorted, the AI recommended buying a domain for the product. I asked whether I could use the name first. About 50 minutes later it recommended buying again, and I asked for the name check. The check found the name registered as a trade mark in Australia, so I renamed the product.

  3. 7 November 2026

    Wording that read like slogans

    On SwimSorted, the AI kept writing slogan-style copy. I told it the whole business had to read as if I wrote it. The result was a rewrite, and a build check that fails if that style comes back.

KeaClip's final review used six AI reviewers, each followed by a second agent trying to disprove its findings. 21 findings were confirmed and fixed. These were AI checks of AI work, not a review by a person.
